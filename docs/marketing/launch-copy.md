### Variant A — the build story
**Title:** PEDMAS - an adaptive math platform that places kids per-strand, not per-grade

I'm a parent in the Toronto area and I spent the last year building PEDMAS,
a mastery-based math platform for Grades 1–12.

The thing I couldn't find anywhere: placement that treats a kid as more than one
number. A "Grade 6" kid is usually Grade 7 in one strand and Grade 5 in another.
PEDMAS runs an adaptive placement per strand (number sense, operations,
fractions, geometry…), starts at the earliest gap, then teaches one skill at a
time — a written lesson, practice, and it won't advance until the skill is held
across sessions. Spaced review brings skills back at 2 days / 1 week / 3 weeks.

Current state: 132 lessons, 634 skills, 107 lesson videos, solo-built
(Next.js + Postgres). $11.99/mo after a 7-day trial.

There's a full sample lesson with no signup if you want to judge the actual
teaching: https://www.pedmas.com/sample-lesson

Happy to answer anything about the mastery model or the placement algorithm.

### Variant B — the technical/problem angle (also the Show HN draft)
**Title (Show HN):** Show HN: PEDMAS – mastery-based K-12 math with per-strand placement
**Title (r/SideProject):** PEDMAS - I built the Kumon method as software (mastery-gated K-12 math)

Kumon's core idea is good — small steps, daily practice, don't move on until
it's mastered — but it costs $150–200/month per subject and requires driving to
a centre. I built that loop as software.

How it works: an adaptive placement test scores each math strand separately and
starts the child at the earliest strand with gaps. Each skill = a written lesson
(every one follows the same shape: start from what the kid knows, make the
common mistake and disprove it, state the rule last) + practice + a mastery gate
that requires success across multiple sessions, then spaced review at 2 days,
1 week, 3 weeks.

You can read a complete lesson without an account:
https://www.pedmas.com/sample-lesson — that page is the best 3-minute
explanation of what makes it different.

Solo founder, no VC, Grades 1–12, 132 lessons / 634 skills / 107 videos.
7-day free trial, then $11.99/mo. Feedback wanted, especially from parents.

### Variant C — the honest-launch angle
**Title:** PEDMAS - just opened signups on the math app I built for my own kids' gaps

After months of building and a closed test with a handful of families, I opened
signups for PEDMAS today.

One design decision I'd love feedback on: the app deliberately gives the child
no choices. No skill picker, no game, no map. The placement test decides where
you start; mastery decides when you move. Every other math product I tried lets
kids (or parents) choose what to practice, and in my experience they choose
what's comfortable, not what's broken.

What's in it: Grades 1–12, per-strand adaptive placement, 132 lessons with 107
videos, spaced review, a parent dashboard. $11.99/mo, 7-day trial, and a full
sample lesson that needs no account: https://www.pedmas.com/sample-lesson

Is "no choices" a feature or a bug? Genuinely torn on how far to take it.
